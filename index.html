Two dates, and most teams have only noticed one

11 SEP 2026

Reporting obligations apply

Article 14 binds every in-scope product regardless of when it was placed on the market. Actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident: 24 hours to early warning, 72 hours to notification, then a final report. There is no grandfathering here — this is the carve-back most teams miss.

11 DEC 2027

Full application

Annex I essential requirements, Article 13 manufacturer duties, technical documentation, conformity assessment, CE marking. Products already on the market are pulled back in the moment they undergo a substantial modification — including, in practice, some firmware updates.

Penalties are set against turnover, not against the product. Non-compliance with the essential requirements or Articles 13–14 carries a maximum administrative fine of the higher of €15,000,000 or 2.5% of total worldwide annual turnover.

Questions

Does this make my product compliant?

No, and be careful with anyone who says otherwise. Compliance is a property of your product, your documentation and your processes. This course gets you to the point where you can assess your own position accurately, run a first-pass gap analysis, and brief the right people internally.

Is the certificate accredited?

No. It is a certificate of completion issued by me, and it is worth exactly what that implies. It is included because people like to have one, not because it carries regulatory weight. If you need an accredited qualification, this is not it.

The EN 40000 parts are still drafts. Doesn't that make the course obsolete quickly?

It makes the standards portion time-bound, which is why every edition is dated and states the stage it was verified against. The Regulation itself is in force and stable. The material most likely to move is the presumption-of-conformity status of prEN 40000-1-3 — which is flagged explicitly in the course rather than glossed over. When I update the course for a change in status, buyers get that update.
